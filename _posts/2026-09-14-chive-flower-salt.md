## Chive Flower Salt

**What you'll need**

- 1 cup fresh chive blossoms, gently washed and patted completely dry
- 2 to 3 teaspoons coarse sea salt or flaky salt

**How to make it**

Start by shaking the blossoms outside to knock loose any tiny bugs hiding in the petals, then rinse them gently and pat them completely dry with a paper towel. Any leftover moisture will spoil the mixture, so don't rush this part.

Snip off the tough green stems and pull the individual florets apart from each head.

Place the blossoms and salt into a small food processor, or work them by hand with a mortar and pestle if you'd rather.

Pulse just until the flowers break down and coat themselves in the salt, forming a bright pinkish-purple paste. Stop before it turns into a runny liquid. This part goes faster than you'd expect.

Transfer the paste to a clean glass jar. Keep it in the refrigerator for a savory condiment you'll reach for constantly, or spread it thin on a tray and let it air dry into a shelf-stable chive blossom finishing salt.

![Finished chive flower salt paste in a glass jar](/assets/images/2026/09/chive-flower-salt/chive-flower-salt-jar.jpg)

A cup of blossoms doesn't look like much once it's ground down, and that's the part worth sitting with. You wait for the flowers, you wait again while it cures, and what you get back is small and concentrated and worth more than the sum of what went in. Sounds familiar.
