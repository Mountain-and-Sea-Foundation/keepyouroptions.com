An option is a contract, not a stock. That single distinction is where most beginners get tangled up, so it's worth sitting with before anything else.

## What an Option Actually Grants You

When you buy an option, you're buying the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell a stock at a set price before a set date. That word "right" is the whole game. You're never forced to act. If the trade doesn't go your way, you can simply let the contract expire and walk away, having lost only what you paid for it.

There are two flavors:

- A **call option** gives you the right to *buy* shares at a fixed price.
- A **put option** gives you the right to *sell* shares at a fixed price.

## The Vocabulary You'll Keep Running Into

A few terms show up in nearly every conversation about options:

- **Strike price**: the fixed price at which you can buy or sell.
- **Expiration date**: the last day the contract is valid.
- **Premium**: what you pay to own the option in the first place.
- **In the money / out of the money**: whether exercising the option right now would be profitable.

## Why Traders Use Them at All

Options exist for a handful of overlapping reasons: to speculate on price movement with less capital than buying shares outright, to generate income against stock you already hold, or to hedge a position against a downturn you're worried about. Each of those is really its own strategy with its own risk profile, which is why "options trading" as a phrase covers so much ground.
